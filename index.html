Good English is not about sounding perfect. It is about helping people feel included.

1

Stage 1 · Warm-up · 2 minutes

Read the room.

ACTIVATE

It is a busy afternoon at the university club fair. You are beside the debate society table. A new student is standing alone, holding a campus map.

A

Look beyond the name. The crowded fair, the shared map, the student societies, and the choice to approach someone all shape how we greet them.

What could you say to make this student feel welcome?

Choose one. We’ll think about why it works.

Local language, real choice

At a club fair in Bangladesh, students may greet each other in Bangla first, then switch to English for practice. Choose language to include someone — not to test where they are from.

3·4

Stages 3–4 · Guided practice + main task · 15 minutes

Live the conversation.

GUIDED → MAIN

Four rounds, fifteen minutes. Rounds 1–2 are guided practice — criteria, starters and a model line are provided. Rounds 3–4 are the main task — you take more of the conversation yourself. Nila and Rafi answer every line, and each round is scored so you finish with a report you can show your teacher.

Choose your role

The timer starts with round 1 and can be paused. Rounds are targets, not strict cut-offs — finish your line before you move on.

    Stage 5 · Review · 1 minute

    Check what you noticed.

    CHECK UNDERSTANDING

    Three quick decisions about the conversation you just had. This is review, not a test — you get the reason for each answer.

    1. Nila says: “I don’t know anyone here yet.” What is the kindest reply?
    2. Which line introduces two people clearly?
    3. Which line closes the conversation warmly?
